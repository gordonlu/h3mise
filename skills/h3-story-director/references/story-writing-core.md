# H3 Story Writing Core

Use this reference when creating or repairing the story before shot planning.

The goal is not a conventional screenplay. The goal is a short-form story whose important changes can later become clear MiniMax H3 shots.

## 1. Start With A Causal Story

Before writing beats, make the story retellable in plain language:

```text
who wants what
-> why action is required now
-> what blocks them
-> what they try
-> what that causes
-> how pressure changes their strategy
-> what final choice they make
-> what it costs or changes
-> what pays off
```

Hard rules:

- The protagonist must materially affect the outcome.
- Coincidence may create trouble, but should not solve the central problem.
- The ending must grow from earlier choices, setup, or changed state.
- A beat must do more than move the character from one place to another.
- If the story works almost unchanged after deleting a beat, that beat is suspect.

## 2. Obvious-Alternative Test

For every major decision, ask:

> What would a reasonable character try because it is easier, safer, cheaper, faster, or socially obvious?

Examples:

- ask;
- call;
- wait;
- leave;
- negotiate;
- refuse;
- take a smaller loss;
- use an already available tool;
- tell the other character the truth.

If one of these solves the problem and nothing blocks it, the story is forcing the character to behave stupidly.

Repair the conditions or the decision. Do not patch the problem with explanatory dialogue after the fact.

## 3. Build Pressure, Not Activity

More actions do not automatically create a stronger story.

Pressure should change the option set.

Useful shifts include:

- a tactic stops working;
- a resource is lost;
- a relationship changes;
- a deadline becomes real;
- new information changes the decision;
- success creates a new cost;
- a safe option becomes unsafe;
- a hidden fact changes the meaning of an earlier event.

Prefer:

```text
attempt
-> consequence
-> changed options
-> new strategy
```

over:

```text
action
-> more action
-> more action
```

## 4. Write For Visible Consequences

MiniMax H3 production benefits from stories that externalize change.

Prefer story information carried by:

- movement;
- possession;
- distance;
- access;
- visible damage;
- object state;
- environment;
- gesture;
- refusal;
- a changed action;
- a changed relationship expressed through behavior.

Weak:

> She finally understands that she cannot keep living this way.

Stronger:

> She places the key back on the table, leaves the suitcase closed, and walks out without taking it.

Internal meaning is allowed, but the production plan should have a visible carrier.

## 5. Dialogue Is Action

When dialogue is needed, each consequential line should have a job:

- obtain;
- conceal;
- test;
- force;
- delay;
- bargain;
- protect;
- attack;
- withdraw;
- change the other person's next move.

Avoid dialogue whose only job is to explain:

- the theme;
- backstory the characters already know;
- what the viewer can already see;
- why the writer needs the next beat.

Natural interruption, silence, evasion, incomplete answers, and misunderstanding are allowed when they change behavior or pressure.

## 6. Avoid Generated Neatness

Reject drafts where:

- every object obviously exists for a later payoff;
- every character speaks with the same concise intelligence;
- every beat cleanly completes one checklist item;
- all conflict is solved through efficient rational behavior;
- the ending closes every loose end without residue;
- every scene announces its theme.

Allow ordinary friction, mistakes, self-protection, delay, and incomplete closure when they belong to the characters and setting.

## 7. StoryBeat Contract

A production-ready StoryBeat should contain:

```text
dramatic purpose
entry state
pressure / trigger
character action or refusal
result
exit state
```

The exit state must change at least one meaningful thing:

- fact;
- knowledge;
- goal;
- relationship;
- commitment;
- possession;
- location;
- access;
- physical condition;
- danger;
- available option;
- cost.

A StoryBeat is not automatically a Shot.

## Final Story Gate

Before H3 adaptation:

- Can the story be retold without internal notes?
- Does the protagonist drive meaningful events?
- Is "why now" clear?
- Are major choices believable under the obvious-alternative test?
- Does pressure cause at least one strategy or value shift when the format supports it?
- Does the ending pay off prior setup, action, or choice?
- Does every major beat create a meaningful state change?
- Are important meanings expressible through visible behavior or a visible carrier?

If not, repair the earliest broken layer before planning shots.
